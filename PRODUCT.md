# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Today, Eggo is a personal tool for the owner and their household (collection owners
like "Ryan" and "Alyssa") to track the Lego sets they own. It is built, though, with
the intent to eventually open up to other Lego collectors as a real product, so
patterns should hold up when membership grows beyond a single household.

Users come to Eggo in two distinct modes:

- **Maintaining** — quickly logging a new set or updating a set's build status. This
  should feel like a 10-second task, not a data-entry chore.
- **Browsing** — looking through the collection for pleasure, recalling what they own,
  or sharing a public view with someone else.

## Product Purpose

Eggo is a catalog for a personal Lego collection. It records what you own, when and
why you received each set, its build status, and theme metadata pulled from Brickset
or Rebrickable. Real-time sync keeps it current across devices, and public share links
let owners show a curated view of their collection.

Success has two equal halves: maintaining the catalog is effortless and frictionless,
and browsing the catalog is a genuine pleasure, a well-made gallery the owner is proud
to open and to share.

## Brand Personality

Editorial and crafted. Eggo should feel like a well-designed catalog or gallery, not a
utility: typographic, considered, quietly refined. The voice is calm and unfussy, never
cute, never corporate. The product leans on the inherent beauty of the sets themselves
rather than on decoration. Three words: **curated, calm, considered.**

## Anti-references

Eggo should explicitly NOT look like:

- **A generic SaaS dashboard** — no hero-metric templates, no identical icon-card grids,
  no gradient accents, none of the default AI-app aesthetic.
- **A childish toy-store** — no primary-color overload, no bubbly fonts, no cartoon
  styling or Lego-branded kitsch. The subject is playful; the interface is not.
- **A spreadsheet or database tool** — not a cold, all-table, Airtable-style grid. Sets
  have images and warmth; the catalog should show them.
- **A cluttered marketplace** — none of the busy, ad-dense, visually noisy listing style
  of BrickLink or eBay.

## Design Principles

1. **The collection is the subject.** The interface recedes so the sets, their images,
   and their stories take focus. Chrome stays quiet.
2. **Catalog, not dashboard.** Browsing should feel like flipping through a curated
   gallery, with editorial rhythm and considered typography, not reading a metrics panel.
3. **Frictionless capture.** Adding or updating a set is a fast, low-effort task. Auto-fill
   from providers, sensible defaults, and minimal required input keep maintenance painless.
4. **Restrained warmth.** Refined and editorial, never childish and never cold. Warmth
   comes from imagery, type, and pacing, not from bright decoration.
5. **Built to outgrow one household.** Eggo is personal today but designed for more
   collectors later; patterns, sharing, and membership should scale gracefully.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: AA contrast ratios, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states,
`prefers-reduced-motion` support, and meaningful labels for assistive technology. No
specific known user needs beyond the standard bar; status colors should still be
distinguishable by more than hue alone where practical.
